Vocation

Every vocation begins with God’s invitation and a person’s generous response. The vocation of the Servite Mission Sisters is a call to consecrated life within society, lived through prayer, service, community, and faithful witness in the midst of the people.

Through the profession of the evangelical counsels of poverty, chastity, and obedience, members dedicate their lives entirely to God while remaining actively present within society, bringing the light of Christ into the realities of everyday life.

We welcome those who feel drawn to explore this way of life and who wish to discern whether God may be calling them to this vocation.

The Servite Family

The Servite Mission Sisters form part of the wider Servite Family, a spiritual family rooted in the tradition of the Servants of Mary and united by devotion to the Blessed Virgin Mary, especially under her title of Our Lady of Sorrows. Although members live their vocations in different ways, all share the same Servite spirit of prayer, service, compassion, community, and fidelity to Christ through the example of Mary.

Servite Friars

Religious men living the Servite vocation through community life, prayer, pastoral ministry, preaching, and service to the Church.

Servite Nuns

Contemplative women dedicated to prayer, silence, and a hidden life centred on God within the monastic tradition of the Servite Order.

Monks

Men living a life of prayer, contemplation, and fraternity within the monastery.

Servite Sisters

Women religious serving through education, healthcare, pastoral ministry, social outreach, and other apostolic works.

Apostolic Sisters

Women living in community and engaged in ministries such as education, healthcare, and missionary service.

Servite Secular Institute

Consecrated women who live the Servite charism within society through prayer, consecration, professional engagement, apostolic service, and Christian witness. The Servite Mission Sisters are the African Regional expression of this vocation.

Lay Servite Order

Lay men and women living the Servite spirituality while remaining active in family, professional, and social responsibilities.

Vocation Journey

Discernment

Each vocation is a response to God's call. If you feel drawn to a deeper commitment of life and service, you are invited to take time to reflect and discern where you may be called.

Every vocation begins with a call from God, often recognised quietly within the heart. Through prayer, reflection, and guidance, we learn to listen more attentively to His voice and discover the unique path He has prepared for us.

Discernment is not about having all the answers immediately. Rather, it is a journey of faith, trust, and openness to God's plan. With patience and prayer, clarity gradually unfolds and the heart becomes more receptive to His invitation.

Prayer for Discernment and Clarity

Lord God, in humility I come before You, seeking light and direction.

If You are calling me to the life of a Servite Mission Sister, grant me the grace to recognise Your voice and to respond with faith and sincerity.

Order my thoughts and desires according to Your will. If this path is truly for me, strengthen my resolve and guide my steps. If it is not, lead me gently in the way You have prepared for me.

Into Your hands I entrust this decision. Keep me always in Your truth and in Your peace.

Blessed Virgin Mary, Mother and guide, accompany me in this journey of discernment. Help me to listen, to understand, and to remain faithful to the will of God.

Amen.

Formation

Formation is a gradual journey of discernment, growth, and preparation for consecrated life.

Formation within the Institute usually takes place over a period of approximately four years.

It unfolds gradually, allowing each person to grow spiritually, mature personally, and deepen her understanding of the vocation and mission of the Institute.

Stage One: Candidacy

1–2 Years

A time of introduction, discernment, and initial formation.

Stage Two: Formation Period

2–3 Years

A time of deeper growth, commitment, and integration of the vocation into daily life.

Stage Three: Profession

Final Commitment

A formal commitment to God through the evangelical counsels of poverty, chastity, and obedience, marking full incorporation into the Institute. Progress through formation depends upon readiness, commitment, maturity, and ongoing discernment.
